## Hero

**H1:** Learn the Game

**Lede:** Magic is a turn-based card game. You and your opponent take turns. Each turn you get resources, play cards, and try to reduce the other player’s life to zero. This lesson covers the shape of a turn and the pieces on the table — not every exception.

**CTA:** Ready to try a pile? → Play

---

## Life and winning

You usually start at **20 life**. When a player’s life reaches **0**, they lose. Some cards change life totals or win in other ways — ignore those for now. Your first games are about playing creatures and attacking.

---

## The zones that matter

- **Library** — your face-down deck. You draw from the top.
- **Hand** — the cards you can choose to play.
- **Battlefield** — cards in play (creatures, lands, and other permanents).
- **Graveyard** — used or destroyed cards. Face up, in a pile.
- **Exile** — removed from the game for now. Less common at the start.

---

## Mana and lands

To play most cards you spend **mana**. The usual way to get mana is to play a **land**, then **tap** it (turn it sideways). A tapped land can’t be used again until your next turn, when it **untaps**.

Basic lands match colours: Plains (white), Island (blue), Swamp (black), Mountain (red), Forest (green). Many cards have a **mana cost** in the top right — that is what you must pay to cast them.

You may play **one land per turn** under the normal rules.

---

## A turn, in order

Most turns follow this shape:

1. **Untap** — stand your tapped cards back up.
2. **Upkeep** — a short window for “start of turn” effects. Often nothing happens here when you’re learning.
3. **Draw** — take the top card of your library into your hand.
4. **Main phase** — play a land, cast creatures and other spells you can afford.
5. **Combat** — choose attackers, opponent chooses blockers, damage happens (see below).
6. **Second main phase** — another chance to play cards after combat.
7. **End step / cleanup** — turn closes; if you have too many cards in hand, you discard down to seven.

You do not have to attack. You do not have to play a land. Passing with unused mana is fine.

---

## Creatures, attacking, blocking

**Creatures** stay on the battlefield and can attack once they are ready. Most creatures have **summoning sickness**: the turn you play them, they can’t attack (they can still block).

When you attack:
- You choose which of your ready creatures attack.
- Your opponent may **block** with their creatures (usually one blocker per attacker when you’re learning).
- **Power** (left number) is damage the creature deals.
- **Toughness** (right number) is how much damage it can take before it dies.

Unblocked attackers deal damage to the defending player’s life. Blocked creatures deal damage to each other.

---

## Casting a spell

1. Take the card from your hand.
2. Pay its mana cost by tapping lands (and other mana sources).
3. The spell happens — a creature enters the battlefield, or an instant/sorcery does its text and then goes to the graveyard.

**Instants** can often be cast on either player’s turn. **Sorceries** and most creatures wait for your main phase. Exact timing gets richer later; for Pool Party and kitchen-table games, “play creatures and sorceries on your turn, save instants for surprises” is enough.
